# Design: The stones identity

River's mark since 2026-10-14: three soft stones inspired by a bold lowercase r, which morph into three level bars while River listens. It replaces the pixel r of [pixel-identity.md](pixel-identity.md) on every surface: the floating panel, the menu bar, and the app icon. The concept is the maintainer's: **water running over natural stones**. The study page that settled every value below is [studies/stones-motion.html](studies/stones-motion.html); the shapes are generated by [studies/stones-mark.py](studies/stones-mark.py).

## The mark

- **Shape.** A bold monospace lowercase r with its slab serifs removed, cut twice: the stem cut rises 35° to the right and lands exactly on the arm's root; the arm cut leans forward at 70°. Both cuts are 0.3 of the stem wide, and every piece takes a corner radius of 0.2 of the stem. The pieces are the foot, the hook (upper stem and shoulder, one piece), and the terminal.
  **Why:** the pieces lean the way text flows, and the cuts read as water between stones. Landing the stem cut on the root leaves no sliver of straight edge for the rounding to turn into a wave; a detached arm reads as a blob, so the shoulder stays with the stem.
- **Size and centering.** The mark is drawn 30 pt tall in the 48 pt panel. The r is narrower than the bars and sits to their right in the source glyph, so the whole mark slides sideways with the morph (about 3 pt): the r is centered at rest and the bars are centered once landed. **Why:** filling the full 34 pt with the r pushed right read as crammed and off center (maintainer, 2026-10-14); moving the r's own geometry instead brought the foot and the hook back into contact at full level, which the overlap test caught.
- **Code.** `StoneMark` holds the outlines (flattened by the generator and embedded as data), the bars, and the morph geometry; `StoneMarkPresentation` and `StoneMarkFrame` hold the motion as pure, tested functions; `StoneMarkRenderer` draws one frame in the panel.

## Motion

| Moment | What the mark does |
|---|---|
| Press | The panel fades in on the r and holds it **350 ms** |
| Into listening | Each stone morphs into its bar on its own critically damped spring (response 0.24 s), the three **40 ms** apart, left to right. Sideways motion leads and growth lags, so the foot clears the stem's column before the hook grows down |
| Listening | Three bars, the stem's width, tops slanted 35° like the stem cut. Each is its own readout: own sampling clock, gain, and spread; fast rise (about 0.05 s), slow fall (about 0.25 s). The level is loudness in dBFS between the capture trim's floor and −28 dBFS |
| A pause | The bars sit at a stone's height and **hold still** |
| Release | The morph runs back, right to left, at once; the r holds through transcribing until the panel fades |
| Preparing model | The r, held still |

Rules the study taught:

- **Motion means sound.** In a pause the bars do not bob. **Why:** a dead or muted microphone must show as bars that don't move while you talk (decided 2026-10-14, replacing a gentle bob).
- **A bar reaches the voice only as its stone lands.** During the morph a bar's height is the level times progress⁴. **Why:** without it the foot, growing into a tall bar, rose into the hook mid-morph whenever the level was above about 0.2 (25–67 ms of overlap, measured frame by frame at 120 fps on 2026-10-14). With it every level keeps the clearance of silence (at least 5.8 design units). A test holds this.
- **No ripple, highlight, shimmer, crest, or roll.** **Why:** a ripple suits motion that spreads or attacks, not a shape condensing into a low bar, and the maintainer ruled out every travelling-light effect (2026-10-14). The pixel identity's transcribing crest and dither are gone.
- **Reduce Motion** swaps the r and the bars at the bottom of a 0.24 s dip, after the same hold; the bars follow the smoothed level without randomness.

## Color

In the panel the mark is `accentDark` from the press until the panel fades, the r included; while the model is preparing, the r waits in `ink`, since nothing is being dictated. **Why:** the accent now means **River is on**, recording or transcribing, a revision of [color.md](color.md)'s earlier "your voice" rule; the maintainer reads one color for "River is active", and the panel's motion, not its color, tells listening from transcribing.

In the menu bar, Ready is the r and Listening and Transcribing are the same frozen reading of the bars, both template images the system paints white or black like every other icon there. **Why:** the bars first shipped in the accent, an image per Light and Dark mode, but the menu bar takes its look from the wallpaper, not the mode; on device in Light mode over a dark wallpaper the bar was a mid blue-grey, the other icons white, and the light accent about 1.5:1 (maintainer, 2026-10-15; [color.md](color.md)). The warning, downloading, and loading states keep their SF Symbols (maintainer, 2026-10-14). The app icon is the r at rest in `ink` on the charcoal squircle (maintainer, 2026-10-15), drawn from the same shapes. At 16 px the cuts close to under a pixel, so the smallest Finder sizes read as a solid r.

## Panel interaction (spike, 2026-10-14)

Run twice on device by the maintainer while typing in Notes: a panel configured exactly like River's (`NonActivatingPanel`, `.nonactivatingPanel`, never key or main) but taking the mouse receives SwiftUI and AppKit hover, button clicks, a switch, and a clipboard copy, each on the first click, while typing keeps landing in the app underneath and River never activates. A plain `NSHostingView` is enough; `acceptsFirstMouse` made no difference. Not yet tested: dragging the panel, full-screen Spaces, and secure-input fields. This clears the way for an expandable transcript and Insert / Copy controls in the panel.
